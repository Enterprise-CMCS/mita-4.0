MITA 4.0 Maturity Criteria

Browse MITA Maturity Criteria

MITA maturity criteria define what "good" looks like across Business, Information, and Technical architecture — at each of five progressive levels from Initial to Optimizing. Whether you're a Medicaid program leader planning your next capability investment, a CIO mapping your data management maturity, or a CTO charting a path for technical capability growth, these criteria are the foundation of your State Self-Assessment.

Used in Step 4 of the Companion Guide

Maturity criteria are the backbone of your State Self-Assessment

In Step 4 of the MITA Companion Guide — Document Your State Self-Assessment — your agency scores each capability against these criteria to establish a current-state maturity profile. That profile is what drives everything downstream: it identifies which capability investments to prioritize, positions your agency for enhanced federal funding eligibility, and creates the evidence base CMS expects when reviewing alignment with priority outcomes.

For Medicaid Directors, this means the SS-A translates directly into a defensible, outcome-linked case for targeted IT investment — backed by a nationally recognized framework.

What the criteria cover

Three architectures. One enterprise model.

MITA 4.0 maturity criteria span Business, Information, and Technical architecture — three integrated domains that together define a coherent Medicaid enterprise. No domain operates in isolation: business capabilities depend on quality information assets, and both depend on a capable technical foundation. The criteria below give every role — from Medicaid Director to CIO to CTO — a common language for assessing current state and planning a path forward.

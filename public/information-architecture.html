MITA 4.0 Information Architecture

Information architecture in one view

A condensed guide to how MITA 4.0 organizes information assets, viewpoints, reference models, maturity, and crosswalks into a repeatable planning discipline.

Jump to overview Key outputs Open data crosswalks

Overview

What to know first

MITA 4.0 treats information architecture as part of capability planning, not a late-stage documentation exercise. The core flow is simple: identify baseline assets, define the target state, compare the two, and turn the gaps into architecture requirements.

Purpose

Give teams a shared information vocabulary

Catalogs, glossaries, dictionaries, matrices, and models make information assets understandable across business, technical, and governance teams.

Method

Connect IA to capability change

Information assets are assessed through baseline maturity, target maturity, metadata, classification, gap analysis, and requirement definition.

Outcome

Make modernization decisions easier

The reference model and crosswalks help teams compare different source structures and align them to a common architecture language.

Process

The IA process, condensed

Baseline

Start by identifying what exists

  • Inventory business terms, data assets, databases, files, reports, and measures.
  • Collect metadata, data dictionaries, ownership, stakeholder roles, and structural details.
  • Assess current maturity across governance, quality, security, integration, modeling, and management practices.
Target

Then define what needs to change

  • Set target maturity and target information assets for the business capability.
  • Classify baseline assets against the target model and compare the gaps.
  • Turn gaps into architecture requirements that guide modernization work.

Why it matters

A cleaner storyline for MITA 4.0 information architecture

The information architecture materials point to one consistent message: MITA 4.0 is building capability architecture one capability at a time, and information architecture is the part that classifies, organizes, and governs the information assets needed to make those capabilities work.

From concept to use

Information architecture is no longer an afterthought

In the MITA 3.0 sequence, information architecture followed the business process definition. In MITA 4.0, information architecture is tied more directly to enterprise lifecycle work, target-state definition, gap analysis, and the requirements that follow.

From artifact lists to viewpoints

Different stakeholders need different views

The material repeatedly emphasizes viewpoints, catalogs, matrices, and diagrams because no single model can communicate the whole architecture. MITA 4.0 therefore treats artifacts as complementary views rather than isolated documents.

From structure to action

Reference models and maturity make the work usable

The IRM, artifact matrix, process alignment workbook, and maturity criteria work together. One defines the information, one shows how to document it, one maps the work to process steps, and one shows how to assess progress.

What the IA package adds

One architecture language across outcomes, roles, processes, information, and technology

MITA 4.0 uses capability architecture and the ORBIT frame to keep information architecture connected to the rest of the enterprise. That makes it easier to explain why an information artifact matters, who uses it, and where it fits in planning, assessment, and modernization.

MITA 3.0 pattern

Information architecture starts after the business process is defined

  • Identify the data elements needed by the business process.
  • Identify standards needed for sharing across the enterprise.
  • Break the data into subject areas and domains.
  • Develop as-is and to-be conceptual data models, then logical models and data management strategy.
MITA 4.0 pattern

Information architecture becomes part of capability planning and change management

  • Identify baseline information assets and collect their metadata.
  • Assess current maturity and establish target maturity.
  • Define target information assets and target metadata.
  • Classify baseline assets, compare baseline to target, identify gaps, and develop requirements.

Lifecycle view

The IA work fits inside the broader MITA 4.0 lifecycle

1.1

Identify baseline information assets

Determine which information assets are used or produced by the business process and define the baseline landscape before change begins.

1.2

Collect baseline metadata

Gather documentation, definitions, dictionaries, and supporting artifacts so the baseline has enough detail to assess and compare.

1.3

Assess baseline maturity

Evaluate how well existing assets are governed, modeled, classified, secured, and maintained.

2.x

Define target state

Set target maturity, define target information assets, and establish the target metadata needed for future-state business processes.

3.x

Gap analysis and requirements

Classify and compare baseline versus target, identify gaps, and turn those gaps into architecture requirements that can guide modernization work.

Process alignment

Baseline, target, gaps, requirements

The workbook maps individual tasks to viewpoint outputs, which helps make the architecture work trackable rather than conceptual.

  • Baseline tasks include identifying business terms, databases, files, reports, and measures.
  • Metadata tasks include stakeholder identification, dictionary collection, and measure specification.
  • Later steps classify the baseline, compare to the target, document gaps, and define requirements.

Stakeholders

Maturity grows from no identified stakeholders, to defined roles, to assigned and active roles, and finally to monitored engagement and intervention when needed.

Structure

Maturity progresses from little documentation to detailed descriptions, documented relationships, structural standards, and centrally maintained descriptions traced to related rules and lineage.

Vocabulary and rules

Business terms, acronyms, concepts, and policies become progressively more explicit and eventually traceable in a central repository.

Classification, lineage, and performance

The workbook points toward a future state where information assets are classified consistently, lineage is managed end to end, and performance and maturity are actively monitored.

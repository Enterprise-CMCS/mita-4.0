What is Outcomes-based Planning?

Put improved results at the center of MES investments

Outcomes-based planning (OBP) places improved results at the center of Medicaid Enterprise Systems investments. OBP helps states define their needs, the value their system and business investments are expected to deliver, track progress through measurable indicators, and demonstrate return on investment—specifically when seeking enhanced federal financial participation.

OBP is the process of

  • Identifying business challenges faced by a Medicaid program
  • Defining the desired outcomes that address those challenges
  • Establishing clear metrics and milestones to measure progress

Why Outcomes-based Planning Matters

Use CMS-required outcomes as a baseline and define state-specific outcomes

CMS-required outcomes provide a baseline of requirements for an MES, yet states should develop state-specific outcomes to fully address programmatic needs. The OBP process helps states develop outcomes that are measurable, achievable, and responsive to distinct business, operational, or policy challenges.

A good outcome should explain

  1. Change — What are we changing?
  2. Affected Stakeholders — Who and what will it impact?
  3. Benefits — What are the benefits of the change?

Outcome Statement Example

Improve provider satisfaction ratings by 15% within the next six months.

  • Change: improve satisfaction with the Provider Enrollment approval timeline by providing streamlined and automated workflows to resolve issues within the Provider Enrollment application.
  • Affected Stakeholders: providers, provider organizations, state users, and others.
  • Benefits: quicker onboarding of providers, improved provider retention, and decreased staff workload.

Why Outcomes-based Planning Works

A structured, repeatable, and transparent process

  • Consistent and repeatable process
  • Sustainability across teams and time
  • Transparency for communication and consensus
  • Traceability back to the original challenge or opportunity
  • Continuous improvement through review and refinement
  • Efficient resource allocation
  • Improved accountability
  • Enhanced collaboration among stakeholders

Key Objectives of Outcomes-based Planning

What OBP is designed to achieve

Demonstrate the value of system investmentsFrame investments in terms of policy alignment, operational efficiency, access to care, service quality, and measurable business value.
Improve program performance through strategic alignmentAlign vendor activity, solution architecture, and project milestones to Medicaid program goals.
Strengthen organizational readiness and capabilityIntegrate staffing, training, workflows, and change management into the planning approach.
Create a culture of measurement and accountabilityUse metrics, KPIs, and evidence-based management to support continuous improvement.
Enable consistency and reusability across state projectsUse structured templates, reusable language, and shared patterns to reduce duplication and improve alignment.

When to Use Outcomes-based Planning

Common triggers from the source document

1. New system modernization or module implementationUse OBP early to define success in business terms rather than treating implementation as only a technical upgrade.
2. Transition from legacy systems or vendorsUse the transition as a reset point to define transformational goals instead of replicating old functionality.
3. MITA State Self-Assessment resultsUse SS-A findings to prioritize outcome areas based on lower maturity and convert abstract maturity goals into measurable improvement targets.
4. Modernization of cross-module business processesUse OBP to define cross-cutting outcomes that span multiple systems or modules.
5. Shifts in Medicaid policy or strategic directionUse OBP when policy changes create new program needs or new system demands.
6. Operational reviews or performance auditsUse OBP to define focused improvements when reviews identify systemic problems or inefficiencies.
7. Response to ad hoc incidents and corrective actionsUse OBP to shift from reactive fixes to proactive solutions supported by measurable goals.

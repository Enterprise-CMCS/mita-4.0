Welcome to the MITA 4.0 Companion Guide

A guided walkthrough of the MITA 4.0 process

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) Medicaid Information Technology Architecture (MITA) 4.0 helps your State Medicaid Agency align business and information technology strategies to achieve enterprise goals, support CMS and state-prioritized outcomes, align to ORBIT capabilities, and prepare for enhanced funding requirements.

The Companion Guide helps you move through four main steps

  1. Conduct outcomes-based planning
  2. Choose your capabilities
  3. Define your architecture
  4. Document your State Self-Assessment

Scope Your Assessment

Select the MITA 4.0 path that fits your trigger

MITA 4.0 offers a flexible approach with two possible paths. The primary trigger for your MITA effort should inform your path selection.

Path 1 — Targeted SS-A

Use Path 1 when the focus is a specific business need or challenge and the goal is a more actionable roadmap and concept of operations.

  • Modernize or implement a module or system
  • Transition from legacy systems or vendors
  • Respond to Medicaid policy or strategic shifts
  • Address isolated issues from reviews or audits
  • Respond to incidents that revealed capability deficiencies

Path 1 order

Conduct OBP → Choose Capabilities → Define Architecture → Document SS-A → Wrap Up

Path 2 — Full SS-A

Use Path 2 when the goal is a comprehensive enterprise baseline, maturity profile, and roadmap for the full Medicaid Enterprise.

  • Know the maturity of Medicaid business capabilities and gaps
  • Create a baseline of the Medicaid Enterprise
  • Support broad enterprise modernization

Path 2 order

Choose Capabilities → Define Baseline Architecture → Document Current-State SS-A → Revisit Capabilities → Define Target Architecture → Document Future-State SS-A → Conduct OBP → Wrap Up

Following Path 1? Start with Step 1 Following Path 2? Start with Step 2
1 Conduct Outcomes-based Planning

Put improved results at the center of MES investments

Outcomes-based planning places improved results at the center of Medicaid Enterprise Systems investments. It helps define needs, value, measurable indicators, and return on investment.

When to begin

  • Path 1: new modernization effort, vendor transition, policy shift, audit finding, or corrective action
  • Path 2: after documenting the SS-A or when preparing to define future-state outcomes

Objectives

  • Demonstrate the value of system investments
  • Improve program performance through strategic alignment
  • Strengthen organizational readiness and capability
  • Create a culture of measurement and accountability
  • Enable consistency and reusability across state projects

1. Define the business challenge

Identify the business challenge and describe the current state. This may be a new initiative or a problem the SMA is trying to solve.

2. Identify goals

Identify the main concern that explains why the agency has not already reached its goal.

3. Identify factors that cause or worsen the challenge

Focus on specific, observable, real-world issues that can be changed.

4. Summarize the challenge

Capture the concerns and drivers in a clear business challenge statement.

5. Develop desired outcomes

Describe the changes, improvements, or benefits that define the future state. Identify the audience, the needed capability, and the actor responsible for implementing it.

6. Determine how to measure success

Identify success factors, metrics, milestones, and reporting approaches that will show progress and benefits.

7. Define success

Set time frames, target measurements, and a SMART objective that summarizes the definition of success.

2 Choose Your Capabilities

Select the capabilities needed to achieve the outcome or assess the baseline

Choosing capabilities helps align information, technology, and business resources with strategic goals and provides insight into the projects needed to achieve success.

When to begin

  • Path 1: after outcomes and metrics are developed
  • Path 2: when assessing the current state of the SMA

Objectives

  • Improve insight into needed changes and improvements
  • Better understand the projects needed to achieve success
  • Align state capabilities with MITA 4.0 capability areas

1. Map outcomes to MITA Capability Areas

If already defined, map outcomes to each MITA 4.0 Capability Area.

2. Revisit maturity assessments

Document the current and target maturity for the capability areas mapped to outcomes.

3. Create a state-specific capability model

If not already established, use the MITA 4.0 Capability Domains and Capability Areas as the starting point.

4. Update capability mapping

Refresh the mapping as state-specific capabilities are defined and maturity assessments are conducted.

Path guidance

Path 1 completes all applicable sub-steps once. Path 2 usually completes sub-steps 2–4 first, then returns later to complete or update all sub-steps after the current-state SS-A.

3 Define Your Architecture

Document the current state, future state, and the gaps between them

This step defines business, information, and technical architecture for the selected capabilities, aligns those views, and prepares the concept of operations and requirements needed for planning.

When to begin

  • After selecting the desired capabilities

Objectives

  • Document the current state
  • Document the future state
  • Assess maturity and gaps
  • Develop the ConOps and architecture requirements

1. Define Architectural Approach

  • Business Architecture: select viewpoints, catalogs, matrices, diagrams, requirements, reference models, and tools
  • Information Architecture: select data viewpoints, catalogs of building blocks, matrices, diagrams, requirements, reference models, and tools
  • Technical Architecture: identify viewpoints, requirement types, reference models, and tools

2. Define Baseline Capability Architecture

  • Business: document processes, activities, roles, regulations, supporting documentation, performance metrics, and baseline maturity
  • Information: document data usage, assets, governance roles, standards, data dictionaries, quality, and baseline maturity
  • Technical: inventory applications and components, map them to capabilities and processes, identify requirements, standards, gaps, risks, debt, and baseline maturity

3. Define Target Capability Architecture

  • Business: define target maturity, target processes, activities, roles, regulations, and business architecture artifacts
  • Information: define target maturity, target data usage, source data, relationships, governance roles, standards, logical elements, rules, metrics, and target views
  • Technical: define target maturity, technology strategy, future components and hosting, compliance principles, target-state diagrams, non-functional requirements, and debt remediation plans

4. Align Architecture and Compile ConOps

  • Align information to business processes and policies
  • Align information to applications
  • Map technical components to technical capabilities
  • Map applications to business capabilities and processes
  • Map target data lineage to applications
  • Define integration architecture and architectural requirements
  • Develop the concept of operations

5. Perform Gap Analysis

  • Business: compare baseline and target processes, roles, measures, standards, and maturity
  • Information: compare dictionaries, roles, reports, reference data, validation rules, quality measures, and maturity
  • Technical: compare current and target technology, standards, integrations, non-functional needs, resilience, and capability gaps
  • Develop requirements, prioritize gaps, estimate effort and dependencies, validate findings, and shape the roadmap

Path guidance

Path 1 continues directly into target-state work and gap analysis. Path 2 often completes baseline architecture first, documents the current-state SS-A, then returns later to complete target-state work and gap analysis.

4 Document Your State Self-Assessment

Capture maturity results, summarize priorities, and prepare the roadmap

In this step, the SMA documents maturity assessment results in the Maturity Profile, synthesizes findings in the SS-A executive summary, and drafts a roadmap of planned projects to address maturity findings and business challenges.

When to begin

  • After the maturity assessment has been completed as part of defining the capability architecture

Objectives

  • Create the CMS Maturity Profile
  • Help leadership understand current and future-state priorities
  • Set clear priorities aligned to strategic objectives
  • Create a visual roadmap of planned initiatives

1. Document maturity assessment results in a Maturity Profile

Capture all maturity assessments performed during architecture definition. Use the SS-A Tool if desired, generate the CMS-required Maturity Profile, and import the results into MESH for review.

2. Use the SS-A Executive Summary template and Roadmap visual

  • Goals of the initiative or project
  • Prioritized features and functionalities
  • Potential risks and mitigation strategies
  • A visual timeline of initiatives and projects
  • Resources and support needed

3. Review the SS-A Executive Summary with leadership

Submit the executive summary, maturity profile, and draft roadmap to leadership, discuss priorities and maturity findings, and update the roadmap to align with leadership direction.

Wrap Up MITA 4.0

Close the cycle, then repeat as priorities evolve

1. Monitor and update outcomes through the OBP process

Work with subject matter experts to define new outcomes or update existing ones, use metrics to assess progress, and restart the OBP process if goals change or if a new priority emerges.

2. Execute projects to achieve desired outcomes

Initiate projects and periodically assess progress toward achieving the desired results.

3. Refresh the SS-A Executive Summary and repeat the process

Repeat the cycle as needed until desired outcomes are met.

Take note

MITA 4.0 is an iterative cycle. You can return to these steps at any time to support and update enterprise strategic planning.
